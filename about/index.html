About

A checklist for the family tax return

Filing a Swiss tax return means gathering thirty-odd documents from banks, employers, pension funds and insurers — a different set every year, arriving over four months, chased by whoever remembers. This site keeps that list in one place so nobody has to hold it in their head.

It is built for exactly one household. There is no sign-up, no sharing, no multi-tenancy — those would be complexity in service of nobody.

Two purposes

  1. Track the checklist. What has to be collected this year, who is responsible for it, and where each item stands. This is what the site does today.
  2. Hand the files over. A transfer area so the tax advisor can collect the actual PDFs without a year of email attachments. Planned, not built.

How the checklist works

A year at a time

Each tax year is its own checklist. The site always opens on the most recent one, because that is the one being worked on; earlier years stay available in the history exactly as they were collected.

Carrying a year forward

The document list barely changes between years, so a new year starts as a copy of the previous one rather than a blank page. Every carried-over item comes across as outstanding — last year's certificate is not this year's certificate. You can optionally leave out anything that was marked not applicable, and keep or drop the previous comments.

The list stays fully editable for the whole collection process. Real years throw up documents nobody predicted in January.

What a document row holds

Name
What to fetch, in the words the family uses — "Lohnausweis", not "employment income statement schema v2".
Owner
Who is going to get it. Not who it legally belongs to — who is on the hook for chasing it.
Status
Outstanding, requested, received, or not applicable.
Comment
Optional. Where it is, who was chased and when, anything the advisor should know.

Why four statuses

Requested is the one that earns its place: most of the delay in a tax return is waiting on a third party, and a list that cannot distinguish "not started" from "asked the pension fund three weeks ago" sends you chasing the same institution twice.

Not applicable matters too. Deleting a row that does not apply this year loses the knowledge that it was considered — and it will silently vanish from next year's carry-over. Marking it N/A keeps the decision visible and keeps progress honest: the percentage is measured against relevant documents only, so marking something N/A moves the needle forward rather than dragging it down forever.

Marking a year final

When everything is collected and handed over, the year is marked final: it becomes read-only and settles into the history. It can be reopened — tax returns come back with questions — but the lock means a closed year cannot be edited by accident while you are looking for last year's numbers.

Getting things out

Print produces a clean paper or PDF version of the year, without the site chrome. Export downloads the year as JSON — a plain, readable backup that is not locked inside this application.

What you are looking at

This is a front-end prototype. The interface is real and fully interactive, but there is no server: the checklist is seeded with plausible Swiss example data and your changes are saved to localStorage in this browser only. Nothing is uploaded, and the data will not follow you to another device.

All persistence goes through four functions in assets/js/store.js — that is deliberately the only seam that has to change when the backend arrives.

Roadmap

Phase 1 ✓
Front-end prototype: checklist, years, carry-over, finalising, history. This.
Phase 2
Backend on Cloudflare Workers — persistence (D1 or KV), a small JSON API, and household authentication so the list is shared across the family's devices.
Phase 3
File uploads to R2, attached to checklist rows, plus an expiring download link for the tax advisor.
Later
Reminders for documents that have been "requested" too long, a year-on-year diff, and per-item due dates.

The full breakdown lives in docs/implementation-plan.md in the repository, alongside the data model and the design notes.

Privacy

Tax documents are about as sensitive as household data gets. Three rules the build follows: nothing leaves the browser in this phase; when the backend lands, the site is private to the household and not indexed; and files, when they arrive, are shared with the advisor through expiring links rather than being made public.

Built as static HTML, CSS and JavaScript — no framework, no build step — deployed to Cloudflare Workers. Source on GitHub.
